# Function inventory

Generated by `v1.31b/scan_funcs.py` from the unpacked image. Borland Pascal 6
emits one of two prologues for a framed routine:

    55 89 E5        PUSH BP / MOV BP,SP     no locals
    55 8B EC        the same, other encoding
    C8 nn nn 00     ENTER nn,0              with locals

Scanning for those beats guessing an offset. Several segments -- `154d`,
`165a`, `1caa` among them -- do NOT begin on an instruction boundary, so a
disassembly started at `:0000` desyncs and produces convincing nonsense for
the first few lines. Every entry point below is a real frame.

This is a lower bound: a routine that builds no frame at all (the three filter
kernels in `1544`, the chain stubs) will not appear.

## The prologue encoding is a fingerprint

Every segment uses one encoding or the other, never both:

    89E5   1000 1065 1084 109c 116a 116e 11bb 12ba 142f 14b7 14b9 1544
           165a 1723 17cf 1880 188f 1891 1931 193a 19a0 1a17 1b24 1b54
    8BEC   1b6f  1ba1

`1b6f` (Dos) and `1ba1` (System) ship from Borland as prebuilt `.TPU` files,
so they carry whatever their own build used. Everything else was compiled by
JCAB's compiler.

Note that `1891` falls on the `89E5` side even though it is Borland's
`Objects` unit -- because `OBJECTS.PAS` shipped as SOURCE with Turbo Vision
and is compiled by the user, unlike `SYSTEM.TPU` and `DOS.TPU`. That is the
reading that fits both facts; the alternative, that `1891` is JCAB's own
work-alike of `Objects`, is not ruled out by the encoding alone. The evidence
for `Objects` is in `00-map.md` and rests on the `TCollection.Init(1,1)` call
shape and the field offsets, not on the codegen.
